Give an engineer one strangely promising accident, and apparently they’ll spend the next few months trying to turn it into a loudspeaker.  
  
A distributed-mode loudspeaker, built from scratch - with little hope of getting it right? Watch me 🫡  
  
The idea got into my head while I was assembling something far simpler. I had connected a vibration exciter but had nowhere to put it. Seconds later, it was lying on a wooden tabletop—and suddenly, music filled the room.  
  
I was amazed by how good such a makeshift setup sounded. Why not build a stereo system around it? So that’s what I spent the next few months trying to do.  
  
Parasitic vibration modes were everywhere. The sound was muddy 😒 Plywood didn’t work particularly well. MDF was better, but still far from perfect.  
  
I mounted it on an aluminum frame with soft silicone joints. Better. Still muddy. Then I added a sheet of plywood over the MDF, with an opening above the exciter. That made a dramatic difference: the sound became surprisingly close to my studio monitors—except above 5 kHz.  
  
Now I’m hoping to fix that with an aluminum plate between the exciter and the MDF panel.  
The result so far: a 50 × 70 cm, 50-watt loudspeaker. The bass is crazy 😎  
  
It still needs a lot of work though
